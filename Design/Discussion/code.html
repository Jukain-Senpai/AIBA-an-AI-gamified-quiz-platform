Hey everyone! I've been breezing through the Calculus module, but Level 4's final challenge has me stumped. I can't seem to figure out the nested chain rule when it's combined with a trigonometric function inside a square root.

The problem is: f(x) = sqrt(sin(x^2 + 1)). I keep getting lost in the multiple layers of derivatives. Does anyone have a "cheat sheet" or a way to visualize this better? I want to hit Level 10 by tonight!

Great question, Alex! The trick with nested chain rules is to work from the **outside-in**. Think of it like peeling an onion:

  1. The outermost layer is the square root (u^1/2).
  2. The middle layer is the sine function (sin(v)).
  3. The innermost layer is the quadratic (x^2 + 1).

Your result should be: [1 / (2 * sqrt(sin(x^2 + 1)))] * cos(x^2 + 1) * 2x.
